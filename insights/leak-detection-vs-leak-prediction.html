Leak detection vs. leak prediction in liquid-cooled data centers

By John Khazraee, CEO & Founder · September 30, 2026 · 6 min read

Every liquid-cooled data center has some form of leak detection. Very few can tell an operator that a leak is coming. That difference decides whether a failing seal becomes a scheduled repair or an outage.

What leak detection does

Traditional leak detection is built around one moment: liquid touching something it shouldn’t. The common tools are:

  • Leak detection cable laid under raised floors, along pipe runs and around coolant distribution units (CDUs). It alarms when liquid bridges the sensing conductors.
  • Spot sensors placed in drip trays, under manifolds and at low points.
  • BMS and DCIM alarms that trip when a reading crosses a fixed limit.
  • Automatic shutoff valves that isolate a loop once a leak is confirmed.

These tools are necessary. They limit how far water spreads and how long it runs. But they all act after liquid has escaped. By the time a cable alarms, the failure has already happened, and the only question left is how much damage it does.

What leak prediction does

Most liquid failures don’t start as a leak. They start as a change in how the system behaves:

  • a slow pressure drop on one loop as a seal or quick-disconnect begins to weep,
  • a flow change that doesn’t match the pump speed or the IT load,
  • rising pump or valve vibration as a bearing wears or cavitation starts,
  • conductivity and pH drift in the coolant that points to contamination or corrosion.

Leak prediction learns what normal looks like for each loop and flags the abnormal pattern while it is still small. Instead of “water detected under row C,” the alert reads more like “loop B is losing pressure in a way that matches a failing seal; repair it in the next maintenance window.”

Side by side

Leak detectionLeak prediction
Triggers onLiquid contact or a fixed thresholdChanges from each loop’s learned normal
Warning timeNone; the leak has happenedHours to days before failure
Typical responseEmergency isolation and cleanupPlanned repair in a maintenance window

RoleLast line of defenseEarly warning

Why it matters more with AI racks

AI hardware is pushing more facilities to direct-to-chip cold plates, CDUs and, in some cases, immersion. That puts water and coolant much closer to the most expensive equipment in the building, and it multiplies the number of fittings, hoses and quick-disconnects that can fail. When every rack has a liquid loop, every rack is also a plumbing problem.

Insurers already see the cost. Allianz Commercial reports that water damage is the most frequent data center insurance claim, and Swiss Re, citing a 15-year FM review, reports that nearly 24% of data center loss costs are liquid-related.

You need both

Prediction does not replace detection. Detection is the safety net that limits damage when something fails suddenly. Prediction reduces how often the safety net is needed, by catching the slow failures that give off warning signs first. The strongest setup uses existing leak detection as one input and adds a prediction layer on top of the pressure, flow and vibration data most facilities already collect.

Where to start

  1. List every liquid loop and its existing sensors, including what already flows into your BMS, SCADA or DCIM.
  2. Start read-only: baseline normal behavior per loop before changing any controls.
  3. Add clip-on sensors only where there are blind spots, such as pump vibration or ultrasonic flow on critical lines.
  4. Route predictive alerts into the same ticketing system your facilities team already uses.
